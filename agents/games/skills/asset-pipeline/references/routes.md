# Choosing the route

Use constraints and demonstrated local capability at the requested finish. Procedural construction can establish exact form without delivering a convincing final surface; scanned or generated inputs can supply rich surface character without usable articulation. Combine methods when necessary instead of treating the first route as exclusive. These are starting preferences, not a model leaderboard or guarantees.

Choose geometry and appearance independently. An articulated or dimensioned object may use explicit parts with image-guided texturing; an irregular generated form may use a reviewed simple material. Before bespoke surface code, make a brief capability check: existing coherent material sets, direct reference-preserving image edits, material extraction/map prediction, or texturing conditioned on an existing mesh. Select according to the missing feature and supported outputs. A generic image editor produces a color source; it does not automatically supply coherent PBR channels, object-specific wear placement or correct UVs.

| Hardest requirement | Start with | Early falsification test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Common static dressing | Existing library or bounded free search, adapt if needed | Actual scale, full shape, license and map bindings |
| Irregular handmade static form | Generated/scanned base, local material and geometry repair | Silhouette, negative spaces, thin structure and rear |
| Hollow vessel, exact fitting, calibrated instrument | Explicit parametric/editable parts and shared materials | Cross-section, profile, mating seat and material swatch |
| Modular architecture or repeated mechanism | Kit with metric interfaces and stable origins | Two real assemblies with the same components |
| Sculptural hero ornament | Reference-guided generation/sculpt plus cleanup | Defining form at target viewing distance |
| Deforming character or cloth | Specialized topology/rig/simulation workflow | Required deformation and pose before surface polish |
| Foliage or other alpha-heavy asset | Reviewed species/form source and suitable specialized workflow | Silhouette, normals, alpha edges, wind needs and target-engine overdraw |

Do not extend evidence from a static-prop pilot to rigging, vegetation or animation without a representative test. A cabinet that must open belongs to the articulated/explicit route even if an excellent fused static cabinet exists.

A material defect does not automatically justify another mesh. Test a swatch or selected existing surface. A topology defect may require reconstruction even when the hero render looks good. Keep the strong parts of a candidate when selective replacement is feasible.

Use procedural modeling where its controls express the observed form; treat a first authored shader as a hypothesis. Exact parts may need sculpted irregularity or a scan/reference-derived material to reach the requested finish. Conversely, generated meshes may need rebuilt negative spaces, contact regions or articulation. A zero-paid route is useful only if its visual proof passes. Compare the cost of a focused alternate input with further labor on a failing treatment; neither paid generation nor a scanned source guarantees a match.

Choose a route change at the scale of the defect: replace a synthetic surface with a coherent material source, replace regular profile repetition with authored/sculpted shape, or reconstruct a fused generated joint with explicit parts. Preserve constraints and good work. Validate the changed region against the same reference before investing in a complete rebuild.

Useful portable-tool fixtures, when those tools are used: linked meshes with different transforms; a baked atlas on a source with multiple UV channels; a Boolean-engraved flat plate with custom normals; quaternion-based imported rotations; a wide shallow object for framing; rough glass export; physical wood side/end grain; a smooth lathed meridian. Promote a fixture only after a real defect justifies it. Existing project helpers remain project-specific until tested across those cases.

Budget exploration separately from production, including elapsed effort. A routine job should not benchmark every provider. Compare an alternate route only when its expected improvement addresses a named failure. For appearance, use the same region, source authority, scale, lighting and straightforward material binding across the comparison; don't add a new source, UV scheme and custom shading model at once. Recheck endpoint pricing/capabilities instead of hardcoding a past winner's settings into this skill. A route is proven only by its delivered visual evidence, not by the provider's description or an agent's ability to explain the method.

For cost comparisons, distinguish a technically delivered candidate from one that passes visual review. Do not compare cheap unfinished construction to a fully finished reference and report the difference as efficiency. Test a representative feature at final viewing scale before repeating a route across the batch.
