# Sources: short film scripts

## Structure and craft
- Ruth Atkinson (script consultant): enter as if in progress; inciting incident early; 3–4
  beats in a 5–7 min short; pursue goals actively and visually; small meaningful changes;
  mistakes (talking, backstory, set pieces, many locations, time jumps); "drama is life with
  the dull bits cut out".
  - https://ruthatkinson.com/short-screenplays-structure/
- The Film Fund: audience should know what it's about by the end of minute one; one major
  concept; end at or right after the climax; ambiguity acceptable; mistakes (dialogue bloat,
  unnecessary scenes, too many characters and locations).
  - https://www.thefilmfund.co/short-film-structure-how-it-differs-from-feature-films/
- General short-film writing guides (one idea, small cast, 1 page ≈ 1 minute, twist must
  re-contextualize):
  - https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/writing-short-films/
  - https://blog.celtx.com/how-to-write-a-script-for-a-short-film/
  - https://www.finaldraft.com/blog/how-to-write-a-contained-screenplay
- Pixar's 22 Rules of Storytelling (Emma Coats, 2011); the Story Spine originates with improv
  teacher Kenn Adams.
  - https://www.aerogrammestudio.com/2013/03/07/pixars-22-rules-of-storytelling/
- Dan Harmon's Story Circle (You, Need, Go, Search, Find, Take, Return, Change).
  - https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/dan-harmon-story-circle/

## Curation and audience
- Omeleto: emotional engagement, intelligence, clarity; meaningful stakes for a strongly
  defined character; plausible obstacles; arc; general-interest audience; data-driven
  packaging (A/B tested thumbnails and titles); Oscar-qualifying festival requirement.
  - https://nofilmschool.com/omeleto-play-moneyball-your-film
  - https://culture.affinitymagazine.us/omeleto-a-youtube-channel-promoting-award-winning-short-films/
- Raindance: hook within seconds, compelling thumbnails, analytics, behind-the-scenes content.
  - https://raindance.org/7-tips-to-break-the-internet-go-viral-with-your-short-film/

## Filmmakers
- David F. Sandberg: Lights Out (2013/14), under three minutes, no budget, shot at home with
  Lotta Losten; became a 2016 feature; behind-the-scenes videos built his audience; test
  with an audience because you lose objectivity.
  - https://nofilmschool.com/2017/08/david-f-sandberg-short-horror-annabelle
  - https://nofilmschool.com/david-sandberg-advice
- Ryan Connolly (Film Riot): writes sound into scripts; make many small films to test ways of
  conveying an idea.
  - https://nofilmschool.com/filmmaker-ryan-connolly-advice-story
  - https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/ryan-connolly-film-riot

## Micro-stories and Shorts
- Shorts length raised from 60 s to 3 min on 15 Oct 2024; square or vertical up to 3 min.
  - https://variety.com/2024/digital/news/youtube-shorts-maximum-video-length-three-minutes-1236166349/
  - https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en-GB
- Alan Chikin Chow (Alan's Universe): scripted Shorts anthology; USC screenwriting; started
  in two small LA apartments; moved to Roku and Netflix.
  - https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-news/alan-chikin-chow-netflix-series-alans-universe-1236478463/
  - https://newsroom.roku.com/news/2025/03/world-s-most-watched-youtube-shorts-creator-alan-chikin-chow/eepq4h1r-1742825058
- Zach King: "magic style" (visual effect + twist); beginning, middle and end even in
  seconds; ideas "we wished were real as kids".
  - https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/video-creator-to-traditional-media-star-the-zach-king-story/
  - https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/zach-king-stranded-2-interview
- Axmo Deus (1-minute anthology): about 15–20 shots per film; off-screen dialogue to save time.
  - https://nofilmschool.com/one-minute-horror-shorts
- One-minute film as a synthesis exercise in film schools and festivals.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-minute_film

## Evidence notes
- The length/shot/beat table for micro-stories is a working budget derived from the 1 page ≈
  1 minute rule and the 15–20 shots per minute data point, not a published standard.
- The six-word story ("For sale: baby shoes, never worn") is commonly but probably wrongly
  attributed to Hemingway; used here only as a compression exercise.
