# Sources: long-form YouTube scripts

## Packaging and ideas
- Paddy Galloway: idea sets the ceiling; top creators spend ~30% of time on ideation and
  packaging vs ~5% for small creators; title + thumbnail as one; glance test.
  - https://www.colinandsamir.com/resources/the-new-rules-of-youtube-from-paddy-galloway
  - https://podcast.creatorscience.com/paddy-galloway-2/
  - https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1811083499044044951
- Veritasium, "Clickbait is Unreasonably Effective" (2021): product vs packaging; legitbait
  vs clicktraps; the Magnus effect title example; titles/thumbnails changed after publishing.
  - https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S2xHZPH5Sng
  - https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20210830-clickbait-effective/
- MrBeast production memo ("How to Succeed in MrBeast Production", leaked 2024): first
  minute most important; CTR / AVD / AVP; "50 Hours In Ketchup"; re-engagement around
  minute 3; "the best YOUTUBE videos possible". Internal document for entertainment content;
  use only the transferable parts.
  - https://simonwillison.net/2024/Sep/15/how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/
  - https://www.creatorhandbook.net/leaked-document-allegedly-reveals-mrbeasts-secrets-to-youtube-success-the-key-takeaways/

## Learning and explanation
- Derek Muller (Veritasium) PhD research: misconception-first videos felt more confusing
  with improved learning vs clear expository videos. The original summary described
  this as "nearly doubled test scores"; verify the particular study and outcome before using
  that numerical comparison, and do not generalize it to every educational video.
  - https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/how-youtube-star-derek-muller-of-veritasium-is-challenging-scientific/
  - https://www.veritasium.com/publications
- Grant Sanderson (3Blue1Brown): concrete before abstract; examples before frameworks; key
  question first; discovery; narrative; topic over production; get a good mic; niche is fine.
  - https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-grant-sanderson/
  - https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/grant-sanderson
  - https://blog.dropbox.com/topics/work-culture/grant-sanderson-channels-his-passion-for-math-into-marvelously-i

## Craft habits
- Tom Scott: clarity over over-explaining; deep research; public corrections; weekly for
  ten years as escalating improvement.
  - https://simonwillison.net/2024/Jan/2/escalating-streaks/
  - https://async.com/blog/tom-scott-youtube/

## Evidence notes
- The 30% / 5% time split and the minute-3 re-engagement are practitioner claims, not studies.
- Muller's finding comes from a controlled study with first-year physics students; it is the
  strongest evidence in this skill.


## Further creator practices
These are firsthand process descriptions and practitioner recommendations unless explicitly
identified as research. The applications in SKILL.md are a synthesis, not universal formulas.

### Viewer knowledge, explanation prototypes and visual continuity
- Grant Sanderson with David Perell: puzzle videos have a clear start and endpoint; exposition
  requires choosing the audience's starting knowledge, destination and distinct perspective.
  Sample lessons with individuals reveal confusion, excitement and useful explanations.
  Consistent visuals help connect concepts; ambient research notes can seed later videos.
  - https://perell.com/podcast/grant-sanderson-math-for-the-masses/
  - Useful chapters: 49:31 (feedback / sample lessons), 1:00:19 (scripts), 1:05:31 (sketches).
- Sanderson with Dwarkesh Patel: intrinsic mathematical interest need not be justified through
  a forced application; online explanations complement rather than replace active learning.
  - https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/grant-sanderson
- Sanderson, "Make math videos!" (2021): motivated concrete examples, explanatory genre,
  pacing, definitions as discoveries, topic before polish, audio, simple visual tools.
  - https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ojjzXyQCzso
  - Original captions and description inspected. 5:30 universal advice; 8:20–18:30 explanation
    structure / craft; 18:30 animation tools. Most principles transfer beyond math.
- Ben Eater with The Amp Hour: an intended single computer explainer became a component-by-
  component series; he imagined his fourteen-year-old self and selected prerequisites rather
  than teaching all foundations. He also describes practical problems viewers encountered
  replicating the build, underscoring the difference between watching and doing.
  - https://theamphour.com/444-an-interview-with-ben-eater/

### Visual scripts and evidence-led revision
- Cleo Abram with Video Consortium (2022): find the visual that justifies the video medium
  during research; gather interviews / transcripts before writing the ears / eyes / sources
  script; use a host's curiosity purposefully. When an episode's conclusion changed, she
  rewrote its introduction. Repurpose reporting across platforms with an appropriate format,
  rather than assuming the same video asset works everywhere.
  - https://videoconsortium.org/mag/episode-8-cleo-abram
- Marques Brownlee with Ben Thompson (2024): test and establish the product judgment before
  scripting a review; leave more flexibility for unpredictable experiences. Reward viewers
  with distinctive insight or production without distorting the verdict for novelty.
  - https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/
- Michael Stevens with Digiday (2014): record surprising findings, synthesize connections and
  write from genuine curiosity. His described process is individual practice, not proof that
  creators must work alone or avoid scriptwriters.
  - https://digiday.com/media/secret-sauce-behind-youtube-giant-vsauce/

### Payoffs, scripting depth and spoken revision
- George Blackman, former Ali Abdaal scriptwriter: outline payoffs first, setups second, and
  connecting tension / explanation third. Precisely written setups and payoffs can surround
  bullet-point delivery. Read aloud and restore personal phrasing when AI assists structure.
  - https://www.georgeblackman.com/write-on-time?4ffd031a_page=8&96a06427_page=5
  - Relevant entries: September 5, 2025 (writing walkthrough), August 29, 2025 (scripting
    depth), and setup / payoff guidance. The archive contains multiple dated newsletters.
  - His 90–120-second payoff cadence and rapid filming schedule are practitioner suggestions,
    not established requirements for all educational formats.
- Ali Abdaal: listicle, essay and story structures; beginners can start with supporting bullets;
  educational value includes useful action; a relevant end-screen next step can serve viewers.
  - https://aliabdaal.com/youtube/the-ultimate-guide-to-youtube/
- Abdaal's "Fast, Bad, Wrong": outline quickly, refine later, consider showing instead of
  telling, avoid redundant repetition and introduce memorable original frameworks where useful.
  - https://www.linkedin.com/posts/aliabdaal_my-approach-to-making-youtube-videos-has-activity-7030127041962237952-Iq-L
- Abdaal's optional three-version constraint: quick draft, major improvements, final polish.
  - https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ali-abdaal_good-creators-overthink-everything-great-activity-7272937308998279168-t88m
  - This is advice against perfectionism, not a limit on factual verification or needed revisions.

### Fact-checking and visual quality assurance
- Kurzgesagt (2023): statement-level source sheets, recalculated estimates, checks for misleading
  simplifications, expert review and differing informed perspectives on contentious topics.
  Editorial checks continue during illustration / animation, including labels, numbers,
  rotation and biological mechanisms.
  - https://medium.com/@Kurzgesagt/how-research-and-factchecking-work-at-kurzgesagt-f5b239188255
- Their production overview, "How to Make a Kurzgesagt Video in 1200 Hours" (2020): research,
  script, illustration, narration, animation and sound. Adapt the process to the available team;
  the reported hours are not a minimum budget for educational video.
  - https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uFk0mgljtns
- Tom Scott's public correction record demonstrates that script, graphic and interpretive
  errors all require correction; it does not establish a universal production method.
  - https://www.tomscott.com/corrections/

### Learning evidence and analytics
- Muller et al., "Saying the wrong thing: improving learning with multimedia by including
  misconceptions" (2008): controlled physics instruction; the abstract reports greatest
  benefit for low-prior-knowledge students without disadvantage for high-prior-knowledge ones.
  - https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1365-2729.2007.00248.x
  - https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ788046
- Muller's own explanation, "Khan Academy and the Effectiveness of Science Videos" (2011).
  - https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eVtCO84MDj8
- YouTube's official retention guidance: spikes can mean rewatching / sharing OR unclear content
  requiring replay; dips can mean skipping or abandonment. Compare similar lengths and viewer
  segments; graph shapes alone do not establish the cause or measure learning.
  - https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en
- Interpreting tutorial exits through task completion, and using a transfer question or
  explain-back test alongside retention, are applications synthesized from these sources.
