# 2D character rigs in Fusion

Inspect the supplied artwork and requested range of motion before designing controls. Separate head, torso, limbs, hands, eyes, mouth, hair, and flexible accessories only where their motion requires it. Reconstruct hidden overlap behind joints and sleeves, choose a consistent left/right convention, and put pivots at anatomical joints. Check the neutral silhouette before animation.

Use Transform nodes and parented or grouped branches for rigid parts. For soft regions, evaluate native Grid Warp or other available deformation tools on a prepared piece; choose a small controllable deformation region and test the edge at rest and extremes. An AE Puppet mesh or pin property path does not exist by the same name in Fusion. Never label a transformed flat image a Puppet-equivalent deformation. Blackmagic's [Fusion tool reference](https://documents.blackmagicdesign.com/UserManuals/Fusion7_Tool_Reference.pdf) documents Grid Warp's editable and animatable grid; inspect the current Resolve version before depending on an older tool behavior.

For face or body turns, prepare compatible front and three-quarter artwork or coherent projected shapes. A flat rotation alone cannot reveal a cheek, side surface, or hidden eye. Define and test a limited turn range; preserve foreground and background occlusion through intermediate poses. Keep mouth, gaze, blink, and body pose controls independent where requested. A character with only one supplied view may support only a constrained turn unless new artwork is authorized.

Demonstrate the rig with a clean rest pose and a short separate performance. Test each control alone and in combination at representative poses, including limbs crossing the body and flexible parts at maximum bend. Inspect two instances with different poses if independent use is promised. Render a motion interval and verify that joints stay connected, edge fills remain covered, and shading or texture follows the artwork. State any unbuilt control or limited turn range plainly.
