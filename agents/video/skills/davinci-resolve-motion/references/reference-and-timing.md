# Reference motion and timing

Label each visual source as original reference, approved output, or defect screenshot. Inspect the original video at presentation timestamps when available; variable frame rate makes average-fps arithmetic unreliable. Note the last outgoing and first incoming frames of cuts, visible holds, direction changes, contact points, and audio accents. A contact sheet helps navigate, but inspect the decisive consecutive frames and play the gesture at speed.

Give each proposed animation a visible cause and interval. Distinguish scene or camera movement, object transforms, actual deformation, and changing light. Leave ambiguous secondary elements still until evidence supports movement. Preserve a cut when the reference cuts: a dissolve or long reposition changes the motion language. Across an action match, compare the outgoing and incoming attention point, pose, scale, direction, and velocity. Check for a duplicate pose, a reset to rest, double visibility, or a one-frame hole.

Set a few keys at anticipation, impact, extrema, reversals, and settle. In Fusion's Spline editor, shape value and timing curves to preserve measured speed; a cut or a deliberate linear move may need no ease. Generic smoothing at every retained key can introduce unwanted stops. Examine the interpolation between keys, not only the keyed poses. Use the Keyframes view to inspect event placement and the Spline view to inspect curves; keep explicit notes on timeline frame versus Fusion composition time when timing clips, trims, speed changes, or a reused composition are involved. Confirm the mapping in the live project rather than assuming the two frame numbers match.

Render the actual timeline at source and final dimensions. Compare corresponding timestamps in full frames and crops. Include the first frame, movement extrema, settled pose, both sides of cuts, and several consecutive frames through fast gestures; watch a real-time preview for rhythm and text readability. For loops, compare both pose and velocity at the seam. Record substitutions and remaining differences without claiming exact fidelity from a few stills. For complex output, inspect the completed file's dimensions, frame rate, duration, and audio alignment.
